ℹ How double-entry accounting works — click to expand

Every journal entry must have at least two lines and the total of all Debit amounts must equal the total of all Credit amounts. This is the fundamental rule of double-entry bookkeeping — one account goes up, another goes down by the same amount.

Which direction increases each account type?
Account Type Increases with Decreases with
Asset (cash, receivables) Debit Credit
Liability (payables) Credit Debit
Income / Revenue Credit Debit
Expense Debit Credit
Common HOA examples
  • Move expense between categories:
    Debit new expense account, Credit old expense account
  • Write off an owner that won't pay:
    Debit Bad Debt Expense, Credit Dues Receivable
  • Record bank interest:
    Debit Cash, Credit Interest Income
  • Reserve project payment:
    Debit Reserve Expense, Credit Reserve Cash
  • Accrue an unpaid expense:
    Debit Expense, Credit Accounts Payable
